# Audit of the UI, UX and features, 18 November 2026

Dylan asked for "an audit on the UI UX and features and how easy it is to understand where
everything lives", compared with Parim's rostering page, and to "make sure scanning and visibility
is one of the most polished components". He also said: don't read the last audit; review the
current website visually and the code base.

**How I read "scanning and visibility".** Scanning: how fast the eye finds what matters on a
screen. Visibility: whether what matters is shown without digging (a click, a closed panel, another
tab, a scroll). Parim's page has no feature called scanning, so I took it as these two qualities.
If you meant something else (for example scanning a QR code at a venue), say so and I'll redo that
part.

## How it was done, and its limits

- **The website, visually.** The live demo on 17 November, the same commit as `main` (`650b51e`,
  confirmed deployed). 388 captures: every office, casual, client and logged-out page at laptop size
  (1,280 x 800) and phone size (390 x 844), plus menus, panels and tabs opened. Logins came from
  Demo phone links; no password was typed. Each page was also measured in the browser: what is on
  the first screen, type sizes, chips, rows, truncation, headings, navigation, contrast.
- **The code base.** Two full maps of where everything lives (the office; the casual app, client
  portal and ways in), an inventory of the shared UI parts, all with file and line references.
- **Parim.** Its rostering page, scheduling page, all-features page, event, client portal,
  compliance, time clock, pay and pricing pages, its mobile app pages, and help-centre articles with
  real product screenshots.
- **Reviewers.** Three reviewers looked at the screens and measurements: scanning in the office;
  scanning and visibility for casuals and clients; visibility and counts in the office. I then
  checked nine of the main findings by hand against the captured text and the code.
- **The last audit was not used.** I had opened its first lines a moment before you said not to;
  nothing from it is in here and no reviewer was given it.
- **Limits, plainly.** I planned 21 reviewers. The usage limit stopped two runs, so 13 did not
  finish: the area-by-area reviews, the first-click test, the click-throughs on a local copy (what
  the product shows after you act), accessibility and speed, and three Parim researchers (the
  schedule help pages, how Parim's own menu is organised, reviews). So: findings outside scanning,
  visibility and where-things-live are thinner than planned; most findings were not checked a
  second time; and Parim's real schedule grid was not seen (its marketing pages only show stylised
  fragments).

## The short version

**The basics are sound.** 0 contrast failures in 31,036 text checks, no sideways scrolling on any
of 186 measured captures, one heading per page, an "Updated" time and a 30-second refresh on every
office page. Individual screens are carefully made. The client's page and an event's staffing page
read at a glance.

**Scanning is not yet one system.** Each screen was polished on its own, so the same fact is
written and placed differently from screen to screen. The eye has to re-learn every page.

1. **One fact, many forms.** How many are booked is written seven ways ("3/4 filled", "3/4 booked",
   "3 of 4 booked", "3 / 4 booked", "Booked 3 of 4"...). Urgent is drawn 12 ways. A session has six
   different row designs. Time ranges are "08:00–16:00" on some pages and "08:00 to 16:00" on
   others, and one Cancellations row puts the date first in one column and the time first in the
   next. A bar that is 75% full is black on Events and amber on the event's own page.
2. **Lists start low and show few rows.** On a laptop Casuals shows 4 of 143 rows on the first
   screen, Sessions 4 of 59, Cancellations 3.5 of 18, because tiles, tabs and filters sit above
   them. Casuals' four big tiles repeat the four tabs right under them.
3. **On a phone, 220px of bars sit above every office heading** (26% of the screen), and the
   dashboard, a session, Casuals and Cancellations show no row of content at all on the first
   screen.
4. **Status sits far from its label.** On the Sessions list the status starts 425 to 480px to the
   right of the session's name, and "Urgent" wraps onto a second line.

**Visibility has four real gaps.**

1. **The numbers don't say what they count.** One screen shows "To fill 48", "All 24", "This week
   19" and "44 places". "Urgent 6" sits 150px above "Urgent 4". They are all correct (places against
   sessions) but nothing says so. "Needs attention 7" and "Needs action 8" are different lists with
   near-identical names.
2. **The dashboard shows one session out of 24** on a laptop and none on a phone. The list of gaps
   is behind an icon with no words. Drop-outs and today's sessions are under the map, below the
   first screen.
3. **You can't see whether an offer arrived or was seen**, and failed or held texts never reach the
   dashboard, Updates or Needs attention. The same session said "Offers are going out." on one page
   and "Not offered to anyone yet." on another at the same time.
4. **The menu hides its own labels and numbers.** A new user's rail is ten icons with no words and
   red dots with no numbers. With names shown it is clear, but that is one click away and not the
   default.

**Where everything lives is learnable but uneven.** The office has 38 pages. 13 of the 20 menu
pages are inside three folds. Three pages answer "what needs me?" with different contents. Settings
are in eight places. Three jobs have no screen at all (add a casual, add a team member, change the
firm's rules). For the casual, six pages are not in the bar, the main pages go by several names
each, and answers from the office live in three places.

**Against Parim.** Parim is a full rostering suite: shift patterns, a time clock with geofencing,
pay and bill rules, absence, invoicing. ShiftDesk is deliberately not that: the firm's planner
keeps the rota. So most of Parim's feature list is "not built, on purpose". What is worth taking
from Parim is how it shows state: one way to write cover, one colour rule, the gap as the only
coloured thing on a row, and the count first on every "action required" row. Details below.

## The plan

Golden rule 10 applies: each wave becomes a phase in `PLAN.md` only when you say so, and anything
that changes how a screen looks gets a mock-up first. Waves A and B are the scanning and visibility
work you asked to be the most polished part.

### Wave A: one way to say it (scanning) — about 1 week, mostly small

1. **One way to write cover**: "3 of 4 booked" and, when short, one amber "1 to fill" tag; green
   "Full" when full. Used on the dashboard, Events, the event page, Sessions, the session page,
   Updates, the casual app and the client portal. One bar: amber while short, green when full, red
   only when urgent.
2. **One Urgent**: the red chip with the triangle, in the same place on every row.
3. **One date and time order**: "Wed 18 Nov, 08:00–16:00" everywhere; the dash for ranges; one
   shared helper in place of about 29 copies.
4. **Say the unit on every count**: "48 places to fill", "24 sessions", "6 urgent places".
5. **One row for a session** (time, role and venue, cover, status, action in fixed columns) on
   Sessions, Events and the dashboard list; status next to the name, not 450px away.
6. **Fix the things that are wrong today**: "Offers are going out." beside "0 casuals offered"; the
   Open shifts day strip that adds up to 10 above "11 shifts"; red numbers on the casual's map that
   are not urgent counts; chips cut off at the edge ("Client rec", "To d").

### Wave B: see it without digging (visibility) — about 2 weeks, mock-up first

1. **Dashboard**: the list of gaps open beside the map by default (urgent first, a "Today" tab),
   with drop-outs and today's sessions on the first screen. On a phone the list comes before the map.
2. **Menu with names and numbers by default**; icons only becomes the choice.
3. **Lists start higher**: compact tiles (or fold them into the tabs) so Casuals, Sessions and
   Cancellations show 8 or more rows on a laptop; sticky column headers; a "gaps only" filter on
   Sessions.
4. **Phone**: the count chips fold into the menu bar so content starts about 120px higher.
5. **Offers you can follow**: sent, seen, no reply, declined, per person on the session page, and a
   real list behind "Offers out 12".
6. **Trouble shows itself**: a failed or held text, and a planner link that has gone quiet, appear
   in Needs attention.
7. **Enquiries opens on the ones that need a person.**
8. **Casual**: next shift and "has the office answered" on the first screen; a licence that is
   running out above the map, not under it; pay and places left above the Take shift bar.

### Wave C: where everything lives — about 2 weeks, mock-up first

1. One "Needs you" page in place of three lists with near-identical names; every count opens the
   list it counts.
2. One Settings home that links to all eight places.
3. Screens for the three jobs that have none: add a casual, add a team member, see and change the
   firm's rules.
4. One name per thing: session, casual, venue, qualification; one label for offering a place.
5. Casual: Open shifts, Urgent, Requests and Help reachable from the bar or one tap from it; answers
   from the office in one place; the booked shift's full page linked from the Booked list; log out
   where people look for it.
6. After login, land on the page the link was for.
7. Bring the two guides back in line with the product.
