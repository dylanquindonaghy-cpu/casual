# Full audit of the build, 11 November 2026, and the plan of action

Dylan asked for "a full audit on the build so far including features UI UX how easy it is to
navigate what needs polishing or improved new ideas and create a plan of action".

## How it was done

- **What was looked at:** the live demo (`main` at `a6bd3c7`, everything up to Phase 35) and the code
  and docs behind it. Phase 36 (client requests, Updates filters, solid menus) was being built at the
  same time, so it is not audited here: 8 findings that Phase 36 already fixes are only credited.
- **Evidence:** 250 page captures (laptop 1,280 x 800 and phone 390 x 844, dark and light, as an
  admin, a resourcer, payroll, a casual and logged out), with measured contrast, tap sizes, headings,
  titles, page weight and timings, plus all visible text.
- **Review:** 16 reviewers, one per area (navigation, dashboard, events, sessions, enquiries,
  updates and records, settings and answers, people, pay, onboarding, the casual's shifts, the rest
  of the casual app) and across the product (accessibility, words, visual consistency, performance,
  task flows, features and readiness). Every finding needs a measurement or a quote.
- **Checking:** a second agent tried to knock down each finding. 66 held as written, 124 held with a
  correction (usually a lower severity or a narrower claim), 1 was wrong and is dropped. 59 were not
  checked a second time because the run hit its usage limit; they are marked *unchecked* below and
  should be read as likely, not proven.
- **Numbers:** 249 findings: 3 critical (all unchecked, all about pilot readiness, not screens),
  42 major, 13 accessibility, 175 minor, 16 notes. 192 are small (half a day or less), 53 medium
  (1 to 3 days), 4 large. The full list, with evidence and the fix for each, is in the audit folder's
  `findings-round1.json` (kept outside the repo: it quotes demo screens and login pages).

## The short version

The product is broad, safe and mostly well made. The basics measure well: 0 contrast failures in
20,548 text checks, no sideways scrolling on any of 222 captures, every casual button at least 24px
(nearly all 44px), buttons that say what happens, and strong guards underneath (tenant isolation on
44 tables, one booking path with a race test, notice evidence for every cancellation).

What lets it down is not broken pieces but **trust and flow**:

1. **A few screens say things that aren't true.** The casual's pay day is shown a week late; a
   cancellation says "cancelled before strikes were turned on" when it wasn't; "No licence in date" is
   shown for people whose SIA licence is fine; Enquiries says "none need a person" above three
   unanswered messages; an "approved" text tells a casual their shift changed when nothing changed.
   Each is a half-day fix, and together they matter most, because one wrong sentence makes a
   resourcer distrust the rest.
2. **The resourcer's main job, filling a gap, dead-ends.** When nobody qualifies, the main button
   still says "Offer to someone" and opens an empty list. Assigning a named person is pick, reload,
   check, repeat. A cancellation record has no "refill this place". Approving a casual's time change
   doesn't change the shift.
3. **Finding things is harder than it should be.** Settings is one 3,456px page; an admin can't add a
   team member; the phone has no search; the laptop rail is 9 unlabelled icons; the main detail pages
   have no tab title; a mistyped office address drops you into the casual app.
4. **It can't run a real pilot yet.** No production server, backups or monitoring; no email
   provider (so no office login works outside the demo); texts are off in production; web push is
   built but switched off; casuals can't book the pilot firm's planner sessions. Gates A and B are
   still open and 13 of 20 blocked items wait on Dylan, not on code.

## The plan of action

Ordered by impact for the effort. Waves 1 to 3 are all small or medium fixes. Golden rule 10 still
applies: each wave becomes a phase in `PLAN.md` before it is built, and anything that changes how a
screen looks gets a mock-up first.

### Wave 0: pilot readiness (Dylan, not code) — start now, in parallel

The reviewers' strongest advice: pause new feature phases after Phase 36 and spend two weeks on the
founder-only list, because no amount of polish matters until a real firm can use it.

- Choose the host and stand up production with backups, one restore drill and an uptime check.
- Choose a UK or EU email provider (SMTP) and send one real office and one casual login link.
- Generate the push keys and switch web push on (free; makes "Tell me about" real).
- Texts in production: build Webex Interact's opt-out webhook so texts don't wait on the reply number.
- The pilot agreement, DPA and solicitor's check (this also unlocks AI sorting of messages).
- The planner portal's API documentation, so casuals can book its sessions (ADR-026's fallback first).
- A one-page service statement for buyers (support hours, what happens if it is down) and security
  headers on the site (a one-line Caddy change, below).

### Wave 2: fill a gap without dead ends — about 2 weeks, mostly medium

- When nobody else can be offered a session, the main button changes to what can be done: chase the
  offers already out, or book someone; the empty list says why ("17 have no SIA licence in date").
- **Book someone** becomes one searchable list that shows who the rules allow, checked as you pick,
  with no reload. One word per meaning: "Offer to someone" and "Book someone" everywhere.
- **Approve applies the change**: "Change to 13:00–18:30 and tell Jamal", "Swap Sam with Olu", each
  with the rule checks shown first and logged with notice. "Approve only" stays as a second choice.
- **Cancellation records link up**: open the session, the casual's page, and "Offer the place".
- **Previews before messages go**: cancelling shows the notice hours and the message; moving a
  session lists who is told and any rule problems before "Move and tell them".
- Enquiries: the reply box sits straight under the conversation while a message waits; the page opens
  on Need a person when it has any.
- New session: role is a choice from the firm's roles, so a typo can't make a session nobody can take.

### Wave 3: easier to find your way — about 2 weeks

- **Settings becomes a hub**: a short side list (General, Venues, Contracts, Automatic answers,
  Integrations, Office team, Data requests) with one "< Settings" way back; forms fold shut.
- **Office team: Add someone** (name, email, role, login link by email, logged). Explain what each
  role can do.
- **How your firm works**: a read-only list of the firm's rules in plain words (offer rounds, urgent
  window, quiet hours, strike notice, data kept for), editable later.
- **Search on a phone** (casual, session or venue), with a "nothing found" that suggests what to try.
- **Rail**: names shown by default on a first visit and at 1,280px and wider; distinct icons for the
  look-alike pairs; one red dot per job, not three.
- **Pop-up menus and slide-in panels**: rebuilt on the Popover API and modal dialogs, so Escape and a
  click outside close them and focus can't wander behind (accessibility).
- **Counts name their units**: "17 sessions", "33 places", and two controls with the same name go to
  the same place.
- **A day view**: every session on a chosen day by venue and post, with booked names and gaps, to
  print or download. The office's most-asked-for sheet today lives in Excel.
- Phone tab rows scroll the chosen tab into view and show that they scroll.

### Wave 4: the casual app on a real phone — about 1 week (mock-up first)

- Home loads the street map only when it scrolls into view, or on "Show streets", and skips it on
  data saver: about 1.7 MB down to under 100 KB first.
- Directions and "Can't make it" clear the tab bar on the first screen.
- "Offered to you" gets a "See and take" button beside "No thanks".
- Home shows only qualifications that need action ("All 6 in date" otherwise); open shifts move up.
- Uploads offer Camera, Photos and Files (and accept a PDF, or say clearly that they don't).
- The pay code step says where the code went and what to do if it doesn't arrive.
- Profile puts everyday settings (days you can't work, how we reach you) near the top.

### Wave 5: payroll can do the job in the app — about 1 week

- Answer one query at a time, or move it to another group; "Something else" is never grouped by week.
- See the casual's pay lines for that week on the query, and a pay records list.
- Staff ID (or the mobile's last 4 digits) next to every name and in the CSV.
- Pay import lists what will change, keeps a history and shows "last imported" on the dashboard.
- Before "Mark as sorted and tell them", show who will be told and the note.

### Wave 6: one look, one set of words — about 1 week, all small

- One `StatusChip` and one `Meter` component, with a check that stops new copies; "booked" is the
  same colour in both apps; one empty state; one way back on detail pages.
- One word each: session (not shift) in the office, booked (not filled), qualification (not
  licence/card/credential) in casual copy; one format for times, dates, pay weeks and phone numbers.
- Texts without en dashes (each reminder goes as 2 texts, not 3 or 4) and with the role and finish
  time; a kinder cancel text ("Sorry … it doesn't count against you").

### Wave 7: records you can get answers from — about 1 week

- The action log gets text search and its own download.
- The notice report gets totals and worker and session filters, and fits a phone.
- Data requests log when a request came in and when it is due (one month).

## New ideas worth considering (for the Parking lot, not yet planned)

- **Day sheet and sign-in sheet**: the day view above, printable per venue, with QR check-in later
  (attendance is marked by hand today, and worked hours don't reach payroll).
- **Worked hours to payroll**: export what was actually worked, closing the loop from offer to pay.
- **Casual-to-casual swaps**: a casual offers their shift to qualified colleagues; the office approves
  in one tap, and the rules and notice are logged as usual.
- **Client weekly summary**: with client logins in Phase 36, a Monday email to each client with fill
  rate, late changes and who covered (numbers only, as agreed).
- **Reliability you can act on**: a "Cancels often" view with the late cancellations behind it, and
  first offers going to the most reliable eligible people (code decides, as now).
- **Office sign-in hardening**: single sign-on (Microsoft 365) or a second factor for admins,
  something a head of digital transformation will ask about.
- **Planner story on the demo**: a seeded planner connection so buyers can see "From your planner"
  and the webhooks working (also in wave 1).
- **A 60-second "what is this" first screen** on the demo instead of a bare password form.

## What works (keep it)

The reviewers credited 136 things with evidence. The ones to protect:

- 0 contrast failures in both themes; no sideways scroll anywhere; every casual button at least 24px.
- Casual flows are short: take a shift in 1 tap, cancel in 3 with the strike shown before the reason,
  ask in 2, pay day without a code.
- Buttons say what will happen, including who gets told; one main action per first screen.
- Old addresses redirect to the right page and tab; 0 broken links in 43.
- The casual app works on poor signal: pages after Home are 3 to 20 KB and kept for no signal;
  double-send guards and clock-change tests are in place.
- Cancellations carry notice, who cancelled and strike state on every row; the record has 20+ fields.
- Secrets are shown once; every destructive admin action has a second step; strikes are framed fairly.
- Imports check before saving; the notice report's CSV is safe to open in Excel.
