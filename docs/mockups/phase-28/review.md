# The staff app: review, competitors, what the office wants, and the look (2026-11-05)

Dylan asked (2026-11-05): "Devise a review of the staff portal review what other competitors features give casuals and implement and execute a plan for adding more features the casuals might want / And what we might want casuals to see as resourcers / Then try to better match the UI to that of the resourcing teams UI use all the skills necessary I've given you", then "Do not prompt me until the full plan is being pull requested" and "Full build".

How this was made: every staff page and the main office pages were captured from the local app with the demo seed (made-up people only) at 390px and 1280px, dark and light (the staff ones, dark at 390px, are in `before/`). Three research agents read competitors' own help centres, feature pages and app-store listings; a second agent per scan opened the sources and kept only what a source supports (the URLs are below). A reviewer read every staff page and its code (the ux-ui-audit method: a measurement or a quote for every finding); another read the docs and the office code for what the office wants casuals to see; another compared the two looks element by element. A planner turned it into Phase 28 (`PLAN.md`), checked by a golden-rules critic and a completeness critic, then revised.

### What works

- Main buttons are full width and at least 52px tall (classes.ts:19, min-h-13); Directions on the pass measures about 51px in staff-home-light-phone.jpg; cancel reasons are 56px (min-h-14).
- Tab bar: five labelled tabs, about 66×56px each when open and about 53×44px when shrunk (tab-bar.tsx h-17/p-1.5, h-13.5/p-1.25). The current tab has a lens, bold text and aria-current, so it isn't marked by colour alone. Counts have spoken words ("Profile, 1 thing needs doing").
- Contrast worked out from the tokens over flat ground: secondary text #55585f on #f2f2f0 is 6.36:1 (6.77:1 on a card); dark #bfbfc4 on #0f0f10 is 10.46:1; the Profile count #5c3f00 on its amber is 8.1:1; "Can't make it?" #b0201f on a card is 6.5:1. Not measured over content scrolling under the glass.
- The cancel flow does what rules 5 and 12 ask. The strike warning comes before the reasons, "Cancel this shift" is the solid red final step, and "Keep my shift" is right there. The confirmation gives the reference, "You gave … notice.", the time recorded and "— Acme Events Shift Team". If the warning goes stale it says "This has changed since you opened it…".
- Take shift books in one tap, and a second tap sends nothing (take-shift-button.tsx `sending` ref). The full-screen "You're booked." gives the day, role, site, reference and sign-off; losing the race says "Someone else took it first. You haven't been booked."
- Pay sits behind a 6-digit code, never called "OTP". Pay disputes become a pay query to payroll with a reference ("Pay query sent.") and never get an AI answer (rule 9). "Don't include bank details." sits next to the box.
- Plain words throughout: no "404", no "OTP". "A strike is a record, not a fine. It never comes off your pay." Every confirmation (request, pay query, booking, cancellation) carries a reference and the firm's sign-off.
- Both themes render every captured page with the same layout, and index.json records errors: [] for all 14 staff pages captured.
- Home and Booked are kept for no signal, with "No signal." and the saved time, and "Taking or cancelling a shift needs signal."
- Help has 14 topics in 48px rows (min-h-12), and its steps name each button word for word.
- The travel area states its privacy plainly: "The office only sees your postcode district (ZZ1), never your full postcode."
- The day strip's key is in words, so colour never carries the meaning alone. Every form waits for the page (useHydrated), so an early tap is never lost.

### What a casual would expect and can't do today

- Call or WhatsApp the office from the app: no number or tap-to-call anywhere (the shifts line number is held back), and no "Message the office" link where the app says "Contact the office".
- Say "I'm running late" or "on my way" on the day: once a shift has started there is no action except "Contact the office".
- Add a booked shift to the phone's calendar (.ics or Google or Apple): none.
- See the pay for a shift before taking it: 5 of 8 demo open shifts say "Pay: ask the office".
- Payslips, take-home (net) pay, year-to-date totals or holiday pay: only "Pay before tax" lines from imported data, and nothing to download.
- Expected earnings for booked upcoming shifts, as a week total: only one shift's estimate, inside Details.
- Open a past shift: see its hours, its pay line, or raise a pay query straight from it (past rows aren't tappable).
- Withdraw or edit a request or a pay query after sending it.
- An in-app sign that the office answered a request or payroll answered a pay query: the Profile count covers only qualifications and the photo, and this firm has no phone notifications.
- Reconfirm a shift ("Still on for tomorrow?") and clock in on site: both parked (docs/plan/parking-lot.md, from Parim).
- Set regular weekly availability (for example "never Sundays") or a weekly hours cap: only single days, up to 60.
- Preferences: preferred sites or roles, and sorting open shifts by pay or distance (the order is fixed: urgent, then date); no map view of open shifts.
- Change her own phone number, email or name: "ask the office".
- Give a shift to an eligible colleague, or see who could swap: only a free-text "Who could take it? (optional)".
- Send a photo in Messages (for example a fit note or a delayed-train screenshot): the composer is text only.
- Read the firm's privacy notice in the app: "Ask the office for a copy." (parked).
- A history of the offers and reminders she was sent by text or WhatsApp.
- Switch between firms when she works for more than one: each firm is a separate portal and login.
- Pages not seen in screenshots and so not audited visually: /pay after the code, /pay/query with the code, /profile/upload/[type] (captured as a 404 because the URL had no type), /login and /l, the cancel sheet, the "You're booked." overlay, and the held, blocked and ask shift states.

### The limits

- Golden rule 1 (AI reads, code decides): every casual-facing figure (eligibility, reliability, strikes, notice hours, pay facts) comes from plain TypeScript over the firm's rows. AI only labels incoming text. A reply is always the app's fixed words, so the AI never writes to a casual. tests/unit/ai.boundary.test.ts fails if the staff pages, portal, notices, notify or message templates import AI code.
- Golden rule 9 and P1-1: pay disputes never get an AI answer; they go to payroll's digest. Casuals see pay facts only from imported pay_records, behind the pay code (30-minute step-up), and nothing is worked out on the pay page. Pushes and texts never carry pay figures. Resourcers never see pay record figures. Payroll never sees strikes, and strikes never touch pay (s.13 ERA 1996).
- Golden rule 2 (tenant isolation): every read goes through withFirm() with RLS forced, and 'their own only' means the worker id from their session. A new table needs firm_id, created_at, .enableRLS(), a TABLE_ACCESS entry, tests/helpers/tenant-rows.ts fixtures and composite (firm_id, x_id) FKs, and cross-tenant tests must pass.
- Never another casual's personal data: session lists, who asked, who was offered, who refilled a place, other casuals' reliability, strikes and travel stay office-only.
- Golden rule 7: seeded fake data only (Acme/Northline, ZZ postcodes, .example addresses). No real employer data until a signed agreement and DPA (Gate B). tests/unit/repo.made-up-data.test.ts. Competitor pictures are never saved or committed.
- Golden rule 5: any new casual action (confirm attendance, dispute a mark) writes an append-only events row. recordEvent() refuses anything the log masker would change: ids, codes, counts, hours and ISO times only. No typed text, postcode, email, phone or a field named 'credential' (use 'qualification' or 'requirements'). The action log isn't purged yet.
- Golden rule 12: office cancels, moves and shortenings always record a reason code and notice hours per worker. A casual-facing record must show the logged values, never recompute them.
- Golden rule 8: messages are signed '[Firm] Shift Team', never an invented name. Golden rule 11: staff copy follows docs/screens.md and docs/messaging-templates.md first (short sentences, buttons say what happens, every action confirmed). Say 'qualifications' or 'licences and cards', never 'credential' (tests/unit/copy.plain-language.test.ts). Template words are kept in step by tests/unit/copy.templates-doc.test.ts.
