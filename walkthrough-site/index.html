Overview

How it fits together

ShiftDesk sits beside what the firm already uses. Nobody has to move their rota. Shifts come in, the rules decide who can work them, casuals are told, and everything is logged.

  1. Shifts come inFrom the firm's planner over a secure connection, or made in the office: one shift, a whole event, or a spreadsheet.
  2. The rules check who can workPlain code checks role, licences, rest, weekly hours and days off. AI never decides who gets a shift.
  3. Casuals are toldA text with a link, plus a notification on their phone if they've turned them on. Urgent gaps first.
  4. First yes winsThe first casual to tap Take shift gets the place. Nobody is ever double-booked.
  5. Everything is loggedEvery offer, booking, change and cancellation, with the notice given. The office sees it at once.

No app to download

It's a web page on the casual's phone, opened from a link. They can put it on their home screen and turn on notifications, like an app.

Code decides, people decide

Who can work, who gets booked and what they're paid all come from the firm's data through fixed rules. Anything unclear goes to a person.

Each firm is its own

Each firm has its own name, colour and web address. A firm's data is walled off in the database, so one firm can never see another's.

One Saturday at Acme Events

Ten steps, from a dropout to payroll. Pick a step, or use Next. Every screen and message below came from the same run of the app.

STEP 01

An urgent gap

The office opens the dashboard. Urgent shifts that aren't filled sit at the top, in red, each with Offer to someone beside it. Four counts above them: urgent gaps, events showing to casuals, cancellations today and enquiries today.

Behind itA shift is urgent when it starts soon or after a dropout. Urgent shifts show first to the casuals who can take them, and their messages go even at night unless the firm turns that off.

STEP 02

Only people who can do it

Offer to someone lists only the casuals the rules allow, with the hours they're booked that week and how they're reached. One click sends the offer.

Behind itThe list comes from the same checks every booking goes through: role, licences in date for the whole shift, no overlap, rest between shifts, weekly hours and days off. Fewest hours that week come first, so work is shared fairly.

STEP 04

Maya's SIA door supervisor licence expired in September. She opens the urgent shift, but she can't take it. The page says exactly why, links to where she can upload her new licence, and lets her ask the office to review.

Behind itLicence rules are data for each sector (SIA, CSCS, PTS, DBS and more), not built into the code. No one in the office can book someone whose licence is out of date.

Wes takes it

Wes's licence is in date. His home page shows the urgent shift under "Urgent: needed now, and you can take them". He taps Take shift and sees "You're booked." with a reference.

Behind itFirst yes wins. A booking is one database step that only succeeds if the place is still free. If two people tap at once, one is booked and the other sees "Just filled".

STEP 06

Every step on record

The action log shows what happened, newest first: who did what, to which shift, and when. It can be filtered by casual, shift, type and dates.

Behind itEvery offer, booking, change, cancellation, licence check and message writes a line to the log. Lines are only ever added, never edited.

STEP 07

A cancellation, no phone call

Zoe can't make her shift. She taps Cancel this shift, picks a reason and confirms. Nobody needs to approve it. Before she confirms, the page tells her whether it will count as a strike.

Behind itCancelling with less than 48 hours' notice gives a strike, which counts for 90 days (both are the firm's settings). Nothing else happens on its own: what to do next is a person's decision.

STEP 08

The office's record

The cancellation is in the office at once, with the notice Zoe gave. It's recorded, never approved or declined. Each record has a PDF for evidence.

Behind itThe notice report lists every offer, booking, move and cancellation with the hours of notice and who started it. That's the evidence the Employment Rights Act 2025 will ask for.

For casual workers

The casual's app

Made for a phone: big buttons, short sentences, and every action ends with a confirmation. Four tabs at the bottom: Home, Shifts, Pay and Profile. Each frame below scrolls; Enlarge shows the whole page.

Log in with a link

No app to download and no password. They type their mobile number and a link arrives on their phone.

  • The link works once, for 15 minutes, and only for them.
  • They stay logged in on that phone for 30 days.
  • The page says the same thing whether or not the number is known, so nobody can fish for staff numbers.
  • Limits stop abuse: 3 links per number every 15 minutes, 10 a day.

Today the login link goes by WhatsApp, and the screen says "WhatsApp or text". With texts only, both change. It's on the list under Where it stands.

Home: what matters now

Home puts the next thing to do first. What shows depends on the person.

  • Anything they need to update, like an expired licence or a staff photo due, in red or amber.
  • Urgent shifts they can take, under "Urgent: needed now".
  • Their next shift in full: times, place, pay, instructions, a map link, Ask for a change and Cancel.
  • Shifts they can apply for, with how far each one is.
  • On an iPhone, a tip to put it on the home screen.

Open shifts, and taking one

Only shifts they're allowed to work, for the next 28 days.

  • Each card shows the role, place, date, times, pay, what it needs, places left and how far it is.
  • Filter by day, site and role.
  • Take shift books it at once: "You're booked." with a reference.
  • If someone else got there first: "Just filled."
  • A shift can be held for people with fewer hours that week first; the card says from what time it opens up.

When they can't take a shift

The page says exactly why, and what to do about it.

  • Each thing the shift needs, with their own status: "You have it", "Expired on 10 Sep: upload it".
  • A plain list of reasons under "You can't take this shift:".
  • Ask the office to review sends it to the office with a reference.

Cancelling a shift

No phone call and no waiting for approval.

  • Before they confirm, a box says whether it will count as a strike.
  • Reasons: I'm unwell, travel problem, family or caring, something else.
  • "Cancelled. Reference …", the notice they gave, and whether a strike was recorded.
  • Afterwards they can tell the office what happened.

Asking for a change

Different times, or a swap with someone else. The office answers.

  • Change the times (new start and finish) or Swap with someone, with an optional note.
  • "Request sent." with a reference.
  • My requests shows each one: Waiting for the office, Approved, Declined or Sorted.
  • They get a text when the office answers.

Pay, behind a code

Pay is private, so it needs a fresh code each time.

  • Send me a code: a 6-digit code to their phone, good for 10 minutes and 5 tries. Then 30 minutes to look.
  • The last 8 weeks from payroll's own records: hours, rate, gross pay and pay date.
  • Raise a pay query: pick the shift or day, then hours missing, wrong rate, paid late or not paid, or something else.
  • "Don't include bank details." Pay queries always go to payroll, never to AI.

Profile, licences and photos

Everything about them that the firm holds, and what's due.

  • Each qualification with its status: Valid, Due soon, Expired.
  • Their staff photo and when it's due (every 36 months by default).
  • Upload a new licence or photo from the phone's camera; the office checks it before it counts.
  • Their strikes, and a way to say what happened.
  • Days they can't work, and how they want to be told about open shifts.
  • Notifications on this phone, on or off.

How far each shift is

Casuals set where they start from and how long they'll travel. Each shift then shows the distance and travel times.

  • Start-from postcode (any postcode, not only home), car, public transport or both, and 30 to 90 minutes.
  • Shifts show "3.1 miles · 12 min by car · 24 min by public transport".
  • The office only sees the postcode district, never the full postcode.
  • It only filters what they see and who gets offers. It never decides who is allowed to work. A firm can switch it off.

Help, dark mode, and each firm's brand

The small things that make it feel like the firm's own app.

  • Help explains every page in plain words, and works before logging in.
  • Dark mode follows the phone's own setting.
  • With no signal, it shows the last copy of their shifts and says when it's from.
  • Each firm has its own name, colour and address: here, Northline Rail Labour in green.

For the firm's admin and owner

Running the firm

The admin's pages, the connection to the firm's planner, and what's set up once per firm.

Office team and settings

Who can log in to the office, and the firm's own settings.

  • Office team: change someone's role, log them out on every device, or turn off their access. The firm always keeps at least one admin.
  • Office staff log in with a link sent to their work email.
  • Settings: the strikes team's email (confirmed by a link first) and each site's postcode.

Data requests

When a casual asks for their data, or asks to be deleted.

  • Find the person, then download everything held about them (JSON, or CSV for Excel).
  • Delete their personal details. Their shifts and cancellations stay, without a name, because the notice report needs them.
  • Download all the firm's data in one file.
  • Every download and deletion goes in the action log.

Connecting the firm's planner

The firm's own planner stays the place sessions are made. It sends them to ShiftDesk, and ShiftDesk sends back who's booked.

  • The admin creates an API key on Integrations. It's shown once; only a fingerprint is kept. Revoke stops it at once.
  • The planner sends a session by its own reference. Sending it again updates it; "cancelled" cancels it.
  • ShiftDesk tells the planner when someone is booked or cancelled, signed so the planner knows it's genuine, and retried if the planner is down.
  • Up to 300 calls a minute per key.

Texts and notifications

Casuals are reached by text, and by a notification on their phone if they've turned notifications on. Every message is signed "[Firm] Shift Team", never with a made-up person's name. These are the real texts from the demo run.

How a casual is told

  1. A text goes first, with a link.
  2. If the text went, a notification goes to each phone where they've turned notifications on. It's free.
  3. Messages that aren't urgent wait for 07:00 if it's night (22:00 to 07:00).
  4. At most 3 offers a day. Every message is logged.
  5. Replying STOP turns texts off; START turns them back on.

The shifts line

Casuals can text the firm's number. Code reads the message for key words and answers at once:

  • cancel: names their next shift and gives the link.
  • shifts, work, available: their own list of shifts they can take.
  • pay, paid, wages: the link to their pay. Never an answer about pay.
  • help, office, talk: passed to a person, with a reference.
  • Anything else goes to a person. AI sorting can be switched on later; it's off now.

Behind the scenes

How it decides

The golden rule: AI reads, code decides. Who can work, who gets booked and what they're paid always come from the firm's data, through plain rules that are tested. The same checks run in the casual's app, the office and the shifts line.

Can this person work this shift? Checked in this order

  1. Not started yet. A shift that has started can't be taken.
  2. Active. Not made inactive or blocked by the office.
  3. The right role, such as steward or door supervisor.
  4. Licences in date for the whole shift, not just today. Never overridden.
  5. "Applied for" allowance, only if the firm allows it, up to its set share.
  6. No overlap with another booking.
  7. Enough rest since their last shift.
  8. Weekly hours under the cap.
  9. Not a day they can't work.
  10. Not blocked from that site.

The office can override rest, weekly hours, days off and "started" with a reason, which is logged. Never a licence.

First yes wins

Booking is one database step that only succeeds while the place is free. Two taps at the same moment: one is booked, the other sees "Just filled". Nobody is double-booked.

Fair offers

For urgent gaps, offers go in small waves to the best-placed casuals: fewest hours that week first, then whoever was offered least recently. The next wave follows if nobody takes it. The office gets an email 3 hours before the start if it's still empty.

Strikes

Cancelling with under 48 hours' notice gives a strike that counts for 90 days. Nothing else happens on its own. A person decides what to do, and can withdraw a strike with a reason.

Where AI fits

AI is off unless the firm switches it on. Then it only sorts shifts-line messages with no key word, and writes a one-line summary for payroll's digest. It has 5 seconds; if it fails, the message goes to a person. It never decides who works or what anyone is paid.

Safety, records and the law

Records and the law

The record of who was offered what, and how much notice they had, is part of the product, not an afterthought.

Employment Rights Act 2025

  • Every offer, booking, move, shortening and cancellation is recorded with the notice in hours and who started it.
  • The notice report flags short notice (under 48 hours, the firm's setting) and downloads as a spreadsheet.
  • Changes by the office are logged the same way as changes by casuals, with a reason.
  • The zero-hours notice rules are expected in 2027; the record is ready for them.

Data protection (UK GDPR)

  • Data requests: download everything held about a person, or delete their personal details.
  • Messages are kept for 90 days, then deleted automatically each night.
  • The office sees a casual's postcode district, never their full postcode.
  • Every download is logged, and refused if it's opened from another website.

Logins

  • Casuals: a link by text, once, for 15 minutes. Logged in for 30 days on that phone.
  • Pay needs a fresh 6-digit code: 10 minutes, 5 tries, then 30 minutes.
  • Office: a link to their work email. Admins can log anyone out on every device.
  • Limits on links and taps, and an alert if a firm sends far more login links than usual.

Firms kept apart

  • Every row in the database belongs to one firm. The database itself refuses to show a firm another firm's rows.
  • Automatic tests try to cross between firms and must fail to.
  • No real people's data is used anywhere in building or testing it.
